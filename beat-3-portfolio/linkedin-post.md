I recently made my first real open-source contribution to CampusOS, a platform built to support different parts of campus life. I improved its loading experience by adding reusable loading states for buttons and content cards, and my pull request was merged into the project.

What looked like a small UI improvement turned into a much better learning experience than I expected.

My first implementation looked correct on the pages I changed, but the production build caught a problem on an unrelated page. I traced the problem, fixed it, and added stronger verification before opening the pull request.

While the PR was open, the project also changed significantly. I had to rebase my work, resolve a conflict with newer code, rerun the project checks, and respond to maintainer feedback before the contribution was merged.

The biggest thing I learned was that contributing to an existing project is about more than writing code. You need to understand the impact of your changes, provide evidence that they work, adapt when the codebase changes, and communicate clearly with maintainers.
